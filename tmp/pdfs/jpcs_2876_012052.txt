An energy management method for combined load forecasting
of fuel cell ships
Jundao Jiang, Liang Zou*, Xingdou Liu, Shuo Pang and Li Zhang
School of Electrical Engineering, Shandong University, Jinan, 250061, China
*Corresponding author’s e-mail: zouliang@sdu.edu.cn
Abstract: The article proposes an energy management method (EMM) for fuel cell ships, which
consists of two parts: load forecasting (LF) and real-time optimized scheduling (RTOS). LF is
composed of Long Short-Term Memory neural networks, which are capable of being trained to
predict future loads based on historical ship load information. RTOS consists of model predictive
control and can allocate energy in real time. Initially, shiploads and battery status are collected
in real time, and then LF passes the predicted load sequence to RTOS. RTOS optimizes real-
time energy allocation based on the predicted load and the current operating status of two sets of
batteries, while meeting system constraints and maintaining the battery SOC at a healthy level,
to minimize operational cost consumption. The simulations demonstrate that the EMM can
consistently maintain SOC at a healthy level and reduce fuel cell ship operating costs to some
extent, thereby improving economic efficiency.
1. Introduction
Currently, the shipping industry plays a crucial role in facilitating global trade [1]. Existing ships mostly
use internal combustion engines powered by fossil fuels, emitting large amounts of greenhouse gases
such as carbon dioxide. It is estimated that by the middle of the 21st century, ships will emit 17% of the
world’s greenhouse gases [2]. In the context of the current “dual carbon” goals, there is an urgent need
for ship technologies with zero greenhouse gas emissions.
In recent years, the application of fuel cells, a green energy source, to the transportation industry has
received extensive research from scholars both domestically and internationally. However, due to their
low rapid-response capabilities, fuel cells alone cannot meet the high dynamic power demands of ships.
This necessitates the coordination with energy storage systems to guarantee stable navigation [3]. The
key to efficient coordination between fuel cells and energy storage systems (ESS) lies in having an
appropriate energy management method (EMM). Currently, the EMM of ships can mainly be classified
into two categories: global planning and real-time optimization. Real-time optimization utilizes
instantaneous online measurements of ship data to generate scheduling plans, which can better respond
to the dynamic changes in shiploads compared to global planning, which relies on long-term historical
data for offline analysis in advance. A three-tier real-time distributed power management system is
proposed, which not only improves fuel efficiency and computational speed but also ensures sufficient
energy reserves even during different sailing modes or faults [4]. Reinforcement learning methods are
utilized for real-time energy scheduling of fuel cell ships, taking into account the dynamic degradation
patterns of fuel cells and batteries [5]. To reduce tracking error and energy storage system losses, a
model predictive control (MPC) method has been developed, and its effectiveness in power fluctuation
mitigation and energy savings has been validated in HESS [6].

The above-mentioned EMMs all perform well under their respective objectives. However, the
majority of real-time optimization methods lack a long-term perspective, which may result in the state
of charge (SOC) of the energy storage equipment falling outside the healthy range. MPC has found
extensive application in the transportation sector as well as other fields, owing to its capability to predict
future steps [7]. However, current MPC models use a constant value to replace future load predictions,
greatly weakening the performance of MPC.
Regarding this issue, a linear prediction method is employed, which not only results in imprecise
prediction results but also has a prediction step size of only one step, making it impossible to make
multi-step predictions and far from meeting the needs of MPC [8]. In [9], ARIMA was used for multi-
step prediction of ship propulsion loads, but it was only applicable to regular changes in propulsion
loads and could not handle the high volatility and uncertainty characteristic of actual ship load
forecasting situations. Similarly, it is unable to meet the demands of MPC in practical applications.
In light of the aforementioned issues, this paper presents an EMM for fuel cell ships integrated with
load prediction. The overall goal is to achieve the minimum operating cost while ensuring energy
demand and sustaining an optimal SOC level of the battery. Specifically, future instantaneous loads
predicted by Long Short-Term Memory neural networks (LSTM) and the current states of two sets of
batteries are used to obtain the optimal energy allocation for the next time step by using MPC under
certain constraints.
The rest parts of this paper are structured in the following. Section 2 introduces the fuel cell and
lithium battery models, mainly FC and LB. Section 3 introduces the composition of the proposed energy
management method. Section 4 conducts simulation verification and analysis of the proposed method
based on the measured data of a fuel cell ship. Section 5 provides conclusions and future research
directions.

The main research objects of this article are fuel cells (FC), lithium batteries (LB), and shiploads in fuel
cell ship power systems. The structure can be seen in Figure 1. The EMM predicts future loads based
on historical load information and then collects operational status data from FC and LB for energy
dispatch. FC and LB provide energy to the ship according to dispatch signals. Additionally, LB can also
absorb energy from FC for self-charging purposes.

3.1. Instantaneous load forecasting
Compared to traditional machine learning models that can only utilize the current time step or a few
recent time steps, LSTM is effective in capturing and maintaining long-term dependencies in sequential
data. It can automatically learn and adjust the weights of memory units based on the characteristics of
input data, allowing them to better adapt to the uncertainty and volatility of shiploads. The particular
structure is depicted in Figure 4, where the yellow box is the input gate, controlling data input; the purple
box is the output gate, jointly determining the current output with the memory unit from the previous
time step; the blue box represents the forget gate, deciding how much information from previous time
step is forgotten.
Figure 4.The structure of LSTM.

integrate seamlessly with shipload forecasting for energy scheduling. MPC effectively adapts to varying
demands in shiploads, utilizing real-time equipment status and predicted load information to solve the
objective function within restrictions. This capability is crucial for real-time energy scheduling on ships.

Based on actual forecasting performance and the speed of the model, in this paper, LSTM gathers
historical load information from the past 30 seconds as input data. After completing training, it outputs
values for the nextN time steps in one go. In this study, N is set to 5, and LSTM performs forward-

rolling prediction. The 200-second forecast results are illustrated in Figure 5. The load profile in this
paragraph includes three main operational conditions of ships: acceleration, deceleration, and
operations. The average absolute error is calculated to be 6.49 kW and the root mean square error is
10.11 kW, which performs well.

4.2. Real-time optimization scheduling
Using the power shown in Figure 5 as the shipload for simulation, the power distribution results obtained
are illustrated in Figure 6. The sum of the output power of the LB and FC is equal to the shipload. During
the operation of the ship, the FC provides the main energy. When the load fluctuates at high frequencies,
the battery provides high-frequency power and can absorb the remaining energy output by the fuel cell,
maintaining the health of its own SOC.
Figure 6. Energy allocation results.
The operating costs of the proposed EMM and the actual energy management strategy are shown in
Table 2. Within a running time of 200 seconds, the total operating cost decreased by about 4%, which
is 0.0159 USD. Meanwhile, Figure 7 depicts the variation curve of battery SOC during actual navigation
over 10 hours. This is mainly because the actual energy management method is not reasonable, with FC
and LB bearing almost equal responsibilities. The proposed EMM aligns with practical needs, where
FC serves as the primary energy source and LB is the auxiliary energy source. This approach, with a
relatively higher CFC, significantly reduces CLB, thereby lowering total costs.

5. Conclusion
This paper proposes an EMM for fuel cell ships’ combined load forecasting. The proposed method
consists of two parts: LF and RTOS. LF predicts the load for future time steps, while RTOS performs
real-time energy allocation based on the predicted load from LF and the current operating status of FC
and LB while ensuring compliance with system restrictions. The simulation validate that the proposed
method can reduce operational costs and improve economic efficiency compared to actual energy
management strategies to some extent. Moreover, it ensures that the SOC of LB remains in a healthy
state. This study offers valuable perspectives for the development of fuel cell ships.
In the future, efforts will focus on finding more precise and efficient load forecasting methods to
enhance the accuracy and speed of predictions. Additionally, attention will be given to the lifespan of
energy supply equipment and dynamic changes during actual operation, aiming to refine the EMM.
