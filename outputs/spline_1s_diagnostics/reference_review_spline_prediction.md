# Reference Review: Spline/Interpolation and Forecasting

This note records how the requested references are relevant to the cubic-spline 1 s reconstruction diagnostic. These papers are not direct evidence that 30 s ship load measurements can be converted into valid measured 1 s forecasting labels.

| paper_title | link | task_type | data_domain | interpolation_method | forecasting_model | sampling_or_time_resolution | key_hyperparameters_if_available | relevance_to_this_project | limitations_for_this_project |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Application Research of Spline Interpolation and ARIMA in the Field of Stock Market Forecasting | https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10759 | Time-series forecasting with interpolation-assisted preprocessing | Stock market index data | Cubic/spline interpolation used with ARIMA-style forecasting | ARIMA combined with spline interpolation | Not a shipboard 1 s load setting | Not enough model hyperparameter detail in the abstract page for this project audit | Supports the narrow idea that spline interpolation can be used to smooth or fill values before forecasting | Stock data are not ship load data; this is not evidence that offline 30 s to 1 s reconstructed labels are valid online high-frequency ground truth |
| Load Forecasting on A Highly Sparse Electrical Load Dataset Using Gaussian Interpolation | https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14069 | Load forecasting under sparse observations | Electrical load / smart-meter style sparse load data | Discusses interpolation baselines including linear, polynomial, spline, and moving average; main method is Gaussian interpolation | Forecasting after interpolation/imputation | Sparse load setting, not native 30 s ship samples reconstructed to 1 s | Abstract-level details only; main reported method is Gaussian interpolation, not cubic spline | Useful background that load datasets with gaps may use interpolation or filling before forecasting | Does not justify treating cubic-spline 1 s reconstruction as measured 1 s ship load; interpolation values do not become true ground truth |
| N-HiTS: Neural Hierarchical Interpolation for Time Series Forecasting | https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12886 | Long-horizon neural time-series forecasting | General benchmark time series | Hierarchical interpolation inside a neural forecasting architecture | N-HiTS | Multi-rate / hierarchical internal representation rather than measurement resampling | Paper defines architectural stacks/blocks, but those settings are not used in this LSTM diagnostic | Shows interpolation can be part of a forecasting model's internal multi-rate representation | It is not cubic-spline resampling of sparse measurements and is not evidence for offline 30 s to 1 s label generation |
| Revisiting Multivariate Time Series Forecasting with Missing Values | https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23494 | Forecasting with missing values and imputation risk | Multivariate time series with missing observations | Imputation/preprocessing methods discussed at framework level | Forecasting under missingness | Missing-value setting, not shipboard 1 s measurement reconstruction | Not used for hyperparameter selection in this project | Provides caution that imputation-then-prediction can alter the data distribution and should not be treated as observed ground truth | Directly warns against overclaiming reconstructed values; this supports labeling spline 1 s data as offline reconstructed and non-causal |

Conclusion: these references allow an offline sensitivity experiment with cubic-spline reconstructed sequences, but they do not support using spline-reconstructed 1 s load as real 1 s measured data or as main paper evidence.
